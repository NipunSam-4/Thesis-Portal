  1. Introduction  
The Integrated  Portal  is a web-based  system  that manages  the thesis  process  for MS(R esearch ) 
and PhD students.  It provides  a single  platform  for thesis  submission,  review,  and approval.  
The portal  is used by students,  supervisors,  academic  committees,  and academic  office  staff.   
Each  user has a defined  role and access  level.   It automates  the currently  manual  processes  and 
improves  clarity,  efficiency,  and tracking.  This document  describes  the software  requirements  of 
the system  and serves  as a reference  for its design,  development,  and use. 
1.1 Purpose  
The purpose  of this document  is to define  the software  requirements  for the Integrated  Portal  for 
Academic  Office.  It describes  the system’s  intended  functionality,  user roles,  and operating  
constraints  in a formal  manner.  
This document  is intended  to serve  as a reference  for developers,  students,  academic  authorities,  
and reviewers  involved  in the design,  implementation,  and evaluation  of the system.  It helps  the 
parties  involved  understand  the system’s  purpose  and what  is included  in its scope.  
1.2 Scope  
The software  product  to be developed  is the Integrated  Portal  for Academic  Office,  a web-based  
academic  workflow  management  system.  
 
The portal  is designed  to manage  the complete  thesis  process  for PhD and MS(Research ) 
students,  starting  from  synopsis  submission  and ending  with thesis  evaluation  including  viva-
voce  and approval.  It supports  online  submission  of forms  and documents,  multi -level  review  
and approval,  examiner  management,  status  tracking,  and generation  of official  certificates.  The 
system  provides  role-based  access  to students,  supervisors,  academic  committees,  and 
administrative  authorities  to ensure  that each user can perform  only the actions  permitted  for 
their role. 
 
The system  will allow  students  to submit  their synopsis  and thesis,  apply  for thesis  submission,  
track  the status  of their submissions,  receive  notifications,  view  reviewer  comments,  and 
download  certificates  after approval.  Supervisors  will be able to initiate and manage  student  
requests,  upload  required  reports,  propose  examiner  lists, and respond  to comments  or rejections.  
Academic  committees  and authorities  such as PSPC,  DPGC,  HoD,  Section  Officers,  Dean  and 
Associate  Dean  of Academic  Affairs,  and Senate  Chairperson  will review  submissions,  provide  
recommendations,  verify  details,  and grant  approvals  as per defined  workflows.   
 
The AR Academic  will use a dedicated  dashboard  to manage  examiner  consent,  thesis  
evaluation,  and related  communications.  The examiners  will use a separate  dashboard  for thesis  
evaluation  by filling  the necessary  forms.  
 
The portal  aims  to reduce  manual  paperwork  and ensure  timely  processing  of thesis -related  
activities  by providing  a centralized,  auditable,  and structured  digital  workflow.  The system  does 
not replace  academic  decision -making ; it only facilitates  the submission,  review,  
communication,  and record -keeping  processes  associated  with thesis  evaluation.

  1.5 Overview  
This document  is organized  to describe  the requirements  of the Integrated  Portal  for Academic  
Office  in a structured  manner.  It provides  both high-level  context  and detailed  system  
requirements.  Section  2 presents  a general  description  of the system,  including  its purpose,  main  
functions,  users,  constraints,  and assumptions.  Section  3 defines  the specific  functional  and non-
functional  requirements  that guide  system  development  and testing.  
2. General  Description  
This section  presents  a high-level  description  of the Integrated  Portal  for Academic  Office.  It 
explains  how the system  fits into the existing  academic  environment,  outlines  its main  functions,  
identifies  its users,  and states  the general  constraints  and assumptions.  This section  is intended  to 
provide  context  only and does not define  detailed  system  requirements.   
2.1 Product  Perspective  
The Integrated  Portal  for the Academic  Office  is a web-based  system  that supports  existing  
academic  rules  and approval  processes.  It does not change  institutional  policies  or decision -
making,  but helps  manage  thesis -related  submissions,  reviews,  and record  keeping.  
The system  works  with the current  academic  setup  and uses existing  login  systems  and official  
email  for communication.  It automates  processes  that are currently  handled  through  paper  forms,  
emails,  or separate  tools.  
2.2 Product  Functions  
At a high level,  the system  provides  the following  functions:  
• Online  submission  of thesis -related  forms  and documents  
• Role -based  authentication,  review,  recommendation,  and approval  workflows  
• Parallel  and sequential  approvals  as defined  by academic  processes  
• Status  tracking  and notifications  for all involved  users  
• Examiner  list management  and communication  support  
• Generation  and storage  of official  academic  records  and certificates  
• Online  thesis  evaluation  management   
Detailed  functional  requirements  are described  in later sections  of this document.

responsibilities  within  the system  are summarized  in the table  below.  Detailed  system  behavior  
and permissions  for each role are specified  later in the functional  requirements  section.

  2.4 General  Constraints  
The system  must  follow  the academic  rules,  approval  hierarchies,  and workflows  defined  by the 
institution.  These  processes  limit  how submissions,  reviews,  and approvals  can be designed  and 
sequenced  within  the system.  
The portal  is required  to be web-based  and accessible  through  standard  web browsers.  It must  
support  role-based  access  control  and maintain  records  of all actions  for audit  purposes.  Data  
storage,  document  handling,  and user access  must  comply  with institu tional  policies  on security  
and data privacy.  
The system  design  is also constrained  by the need  to support  multiple  user roles,  parallel  
reviews,  and confidential  information  handling,  such as restricted  visibility  of examiner  ordering.   
2.5 Assumptions  and Dependencies  
The system  is developed  with the assumption  that users  have  access  to a modern  web browser.  It 
is also assumed  that users  will have  valid  institutional  credentials  to access  the portal.  
The system  depends  on existing  academic  rules,  approval  structures,  and workflows  defined  by 
the institution . 
The system  may rely on external  services  such as institutional  authentication  systems,  email  
services,  and data storage  infrastructure.  Changes  or unavailability  of these  services  can affect  
system  functionality  and may require  updates  to this specificatio n. 
3. Specific  Requirements  
This section  describes  the detailed  requirements  of the Integrated  Portal  for Academic  Office.  
These  requirements  define  what  the system  must  do and how it should  behave.  
Each  requirement  in this section  is written  to be clear,  testable,  and uniquely  identified.  The 
requirements  are organized  into categories  so they are easy to understand  and refer ence. 
This section  focuses  on system  behavior  and constraints  and avoids  describing  design  or 
implementation  details.  
3.1 External  Interface  Requirements  
This section  describes  how the system  interacts  with users,  hardware,  software,  and 
communication  services.  It defines  the external  interfaces  required  for system  operation.  
3.1.1  User  Interfaces  
The system  shall  provide  a web-based  user interface  accessible  through  standard  web browsers.  
The interface  shall  be role-based,  showing  only the functions  and information  relevant  to the 
logged -in user.  Forms,  status  indicators,  and notifications  shall  be presented  in a clear  and 
consistent  manner  to support  ease of use. 
3.1.2  Hardware  Interfaces  
The system  does not require  any specialized  hardware  interfaces.  It shall  operate  on standard  
client  devices  such as desktop  computers,  laptops,  and mobile  devices  with internet  access.

3.2.1  Synopsis  Submission  and Review  – PTS-1  
 
3.2.1.1  Introduction  
PTS-1 supports  the submission  and review  of a student’s  thesis  synopsis  after the open  seminar.  
The system  enables  supervisors,  academic  committees,  and academic  authorities  to review,  
recommend,  revert,  or approve  the synopsis  through  a defined  workflow.  
 
3.2.1.2  Inputs  
• PTS-1 form  details  entered  by the main  supervisor  
• Synopsis  report  uploaded  as a document  
• Review  decisions  (recommend  or revert)  from  reviewers  
• Comments  provided  during  review  or reversion  
 
3.2.1.3  Processing  
• The main  supervisor  initiates  the PTS-1 request  by submitting  the completed  form  with 
the synopsis  report.  
• All co-supervisors  receive  the request  in parallel  and may either  recommend  or revert  the 
submission.  
• If any co-supervisor  reverts  the request,  the system  marks  the PTS-1 status  as Reverted  
and returns  it to the main  supervisor.  
• After  all supervisors  recommend,  the request  is forwarded  to PSPC  members  for review.  
• PSPC  members  review  the request  and may recommend  or revert  it. 
• The request  is then reviewed  sequentially  by DPGC  and the Head  of Department.  
• The Section  Officer  verifies  the submitted  details  and forwards  the request  without  the 
option  to revert.  
• The Dean  of Academic  Affairs  reviews  the request  and makes  the final decision  to 
approve,  reject,  or revert.  
• On approval,  the system  marks  the PTS-1 status  as Accepted . 
 
3.2.1.4  Outputs  
• Updated  PTS-1 status  (In Progress,  Reverted,  Accepted,  or Rejected)

  • Notifications  sent to all relevant  users  on submission,  reversion,  and approval  
• Stored  review  comments  and timestamps  
• Maintained  record  of all submission  attempts  
 
3.2.1.5  Error  Handling  
• If required  fields  or documents  are missing,  the system  shall  prevent  submission  and

3.2.2  Examiner  List Submission  and Approval  – PTS-3 
 
3.2.2.1  Introduction  
PTS-3 supports  the submission,  review,  and approval  of the examiner  list for thesis  evaluation.  
The system  enables  controlled  review,  ordering,  and approval  of examiners  while  enforcing  
confidentiality  rules  based  on user roles.  
 
3.2.2.2  Inputs  
• Examiner  details  submitted  by the main  supervisor  
• Consent  status  for each proposed  examiner  
• Review  decisions  (recommend,  revert,  approve)  
• Comments  provided  during  review  or reversion  
• Examiner  ordering  changes  by authorized  roles  
 
3.2.2.3  Processing  
• The main  supervisor  initiates  PTS-3 by submitting  a proposed  list of examiners  and 
indicating  whether  prior  consent  has been  obtained  for each examiner.  
• Co-supervisors  are granted  view -only access  to the examiner  list and cannot  modify  or 
revert  it. 
• The Head  of Department  reviews  the list and may recommend  or revert  it with 
comments.  
• The Section  Officer  verifies  examiner  details  and forwards  the request  with optional  
comments.  
• The Dean  of Academic  Affairs  reviews  the list and may reorder  examiners,  recommend,  
or revert  the submission.  
• The Senate  Chairperson  reviews  the examiner  list and may reorder,  revert,  or approve  it. 
• Any ordering  changes  made  by the Dean  of Academic  Affairs  or the Senate  Chairperson  
are recorded  by the system  and made  visible  only to them . 
• After  approval,  the finalized  examiner  list is made  available  to the AR Academic  for 
further  processing.  
 
3.2.2.4  Outputs  
• Updated  PTS-3 status  (In Progress,  Reverted,  or Approved)  
• Finalized  examiner  list for academic  processing  
• Stored  examiner  ordering  history  
• Notifications  sent to relevant  users  on submission,  reversion,  or approval

  3.2.3  Thesis  Submission  and Approval  – PTS-2 
 
3.2.3.1  Introduction  
PTS-2 supports  the submission  and approval  of a student’s  thesis.  The system  enables  
supervisors,  academic  authorities,  and administrative  staff to review  the submission  and take 
approval  or reversion  actions  through  a defined  workflow.  
 
3.2.3.2  Inputs  
• PTS-2 form  details  submitted  by the student  
• Thesis  document  uploaded  by the student  
• Review  decisions  (approve  or revert)  from  reviewers  
• Comments  provided  during  reversion  
 
3.2.3.3  Processing  
• The student  completes  the PTS-2 form  and uploads  the thesis  document  through  the 
system.  
• The system  records  the thesis  submission  date.  
• All assigned  supervisors  receive  the submission  in parallel  and may either  approve  or 
revert  it with a comment.  
• If any supervisor  reverts  the submission,  the system  returns  it to the student  for re-
submission.  
• After  supervisor  approvals,  the submission  is reviewed  by the Departmental  Postgraduate  
Committee  and the Head  of Department.  
• The AR Academic  performs  the final review  and may accept  or revert  the submission.  
• Upon  acceptance,  the system  generates  a thesis  submission  certificate.  
 
3.2.3.4  Outputs  
• Updated  PTS-2 status  (In Progress,  Reverted,  or Accepted)  
• Generated  thesis  submission  certificate  in PDF format  
• Download  access  to the certificate  for the student  
• Status  visibility  for all involved  users  
 
3.2.3.5  Error  Handling  
• The system  shall  prevent  submission  if required  fields  or the thesis  document  are missing.  
• Reversion  actions  shall  require  a mandatory  comment.  
• Unauthorized  users  shall  be prevented  from  approving  or reverting  the submission.

3.2.6  Synopsis  Submission  and Review  – MSRTS -1  
 
 
3.2.6 .1 Introduction

3.2.6 .2 Inputs

3.2.6 .3 Processing  
• The main  supervisor  initiates  the MSRTS -1 request  by submitting  the completed  form

option  to revert.

• On approval,  the system  marks  the MSRTS -1 status  as Accepted .

3.4 Non-Functional  Requirements  
This section  defines  the quality  attributes  that the system  must  satisfy  at the overall  system  level.  
3.4.1  Performance  
• The system  shall  respond  to user actions  such as form  submission,  approval,  and status  
updates  within  a few seconds  under  normal  operating  conditions.  
• The system  shall  support  multiple  concurrent  users  without  significant  performance  
degradation  
3.4.2  Reliability  
• The system  shall  operate  without  failure  for extended  periods  during  normal  use. 
• In case of a failure,  no submitted  data shall  be lost. 
3.4.3  Availability  
• The system  shall  be available  24 hours  a day, 7 days a week,  except  during  scheduled  
maintenance.  
3.4.4  Security  
• The system  shall  require  user authentication  using  unique  login  credentials.  
• Access  to system  functions  shall  be restricted  based  on user roles.  
• Basic  protection  shall  be provided  to prevent  unauthorized  access  to user data.  
3.4.5  Maintainability  
• The system  shall  be structured  in a way that allows  future  updates  and bug fixes  without  
impacting  existing  core functionality.  
• Changes  to the system  shall  be possible  without  requiring  major  rework  of unrelated  
components.  
3.4.6  Portability  
• The system  shall  be accessible  through  standard  web browsers  across  common  operating  
systems.  
• The system  shall  be designed  to allow  upgrades  without  loss of existing  user data or 
disruption  of core workflows.  
Integrated  Portal  for Academic  Office  
Software  Requirements  Specification   Page  23 
  3.5 Inverse  Requirements  
The system  shall  not make  academic  decisions  or judgments.  All approvals,  rejections,  and 
recommendations  are made  by authorized  users,  and the system  only records  and routes  these  
actions.  The system  shall  not allow  users  to access  functions  or data outside  their assigned  roles.  
It shall  also not modify  or delete  academic  records  without  proper  authorization.  
3.6 Design  Constraints  
The design  of the system  is constrained  by the need  to support  predefined  academic  workflows,  
multiple  user roles,  and controlled  information  visibility.  These  constraints  affect  how system  
features  can be structured  and presented.  No additional  hardware -specific  or technology -specific  
design  constraints  are imposed  beyond  those  already  stated  in this document.  
3.7 Logical  Database  Requirements  
The system  requires  persistent  storage  to maintain  records  related  to users,  submissions,  
approvals,  comments,  and system  activities.  The stored  data must  support  tracking  of submission  
states  and historical  actions  across  the academic  workflow.  
The database  must  logically  support  structured  academic  records,  document  references,  and 
time-based  information  such as submission  and approval  dates.  Data  retention  and modification  
shall  follow  institutional  practices,  and records  shall  remain  available  for review  and reference  as 
required.  
3.8 Other  Requirements  
The system  shall  provide  basic  search  and filtering  capabilities  to help users  locate  records  
efficiently.  The system  shall  be designed  to allow  future  enhancements  without  major  changes  to 
existing  functionality.
